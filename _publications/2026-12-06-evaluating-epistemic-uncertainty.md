Epistemic uncertainty is usually evaluated through proxy tasks such as out-of-distribution detection and active learning, yet the optimal decision rules for these tasks do not match the scores used to quantify it. Building on the epistemic reject-option framework, this work evaluates epistemic uncertainty by how well it identifies regret, the reducible part of the error. It shows that the optimal selective predictor thresholds a convex combination of aleatoric and epistemic uncertainty, and introduces a benchmark on densely annotated datasets where decision-theoretic rankings of methods differ substantially from proxy-task rankings.
